My research focuses on extreme weather events and their impacts on human health, both now and in the short- and long-term future. I am a UKRI Future Leaders Fellow and I am leading a research programme to develop a new field of weather-health prediction science to enhance the climate resilience of the NHS and other health services.

More broadly, my research includes investigating changes in heatwaves, humidity, cold snaps, and storms between different time scales and climate change scenarios, including counterfactual ones for forming impact attribution statements. I utilise weather and climate observations, weather forecasts, climate model large ensembles, as well as health models, thereby uniquely bridging the gap between climate science and epidemiology. In 2024, my scientific contributions were recognised with three national honours: the L F Richardson Award from the Royal Meteorological Society, the Emerging Leaders Prize from Medical Research UK, and the Impact Award from the Royal Meteorological Society.

I am passionate about generating novel science that also provides vital evidence to climate policymaking. I was a contributing author to the UK Climate Change Risk Assessment (CCRA4) and IPCC Sixth Assessment Report. I also held an honorary position at the UK Health Security Agency for two years. Before joining Bristol, I did a PhD in Atmosphere, Oceans and Climate at the University of Reading (2018)
