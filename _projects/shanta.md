## Aims and outputs

- **Climate and health research:** advance understanding of how climate change affects human health, particularly through extreme heat, flooding, air pollution and other environmental hazards.
- **Climate-resilient communities:** investigate how climate risks interact with urban development, infrastructure, ecosystems and social inequalities, with a focus on Bristol and Mumbai.
- **Planetary health and nature:** explore the connections between environmental change, biodiversity, green spaces and human wellbeing, including opportunities for healthier and more resilient environments.
- **International collaboration:** establish a sustained research partnership between Bristol and Mumbai, supporting interdisciplinary research, early-career researchers and the development of new scientific approaches.
- **Policy and clinical practice:** translate climate and health research into actionable evidence for public health, healthcare systems, urban planning and climate adaptation.

## Methods

The Shanta Planetary Health programme brings together expertise in climate science, epidemiology, medicine, environmental science and social science to investigate how environmental change affects human health and wellbeing. Centred on the University of Bristol's campuses in Bristol and Mumbai, the programme combines physical climate science with health data, interdisciplinary research and engagement with policymakers and practitioners.

Our research uses climate observations, numerical modelling, future projections and climate change attribution to understand how environmental hazards are changing and how these changes affect human populations. We investigate the consequences of extreme heat, flooding, air pollution and wider environmental pressures, alongside the interactions between climate change, biodiversity, the built environment and social inequalities.

A central ambition is to move beyond identifying climate-related hazards towards quantifying their health consequences and developing practical responses. This includes linking environmental exposures with epidemiological and clinical data, identifying vulnerable populations, and evaluating opportunities for adaptation and prevention.

The programme also explores how natural environments, including trees, urban green spaces and healthy ecosystems, can help reduce climate risks while supporting physical and mental wellbeing.

Through the Shanta Fellows in Planetary Health, we are building interdisciplinary collaborations between Bristol and Mumbai, developing new research programmes and strengthening links between academic research, healthcare, policy and practice. Ultimately, our goal is to translate climate science into healthier communities, more resilient health systems and evidence-based responses to planetary health challenges.

## Team

## Shanta Professorial Chair in Planetary Health

### Professor Dann Mitchell
*Climate Change and Human Health*

Professor Dann Mitchell is a climate scientist specialising in the impacts of climate change on human health. He jointly leads the Health and Wellbeing chapter of the UK's Fourth Climate Change Risk Assessment (CCRA4) and has pioneered research in climate–health extreme event attribution, developing methods to quantify the contribution of anthropogenic climate change to observed health impacts.

He directs a major Wellcome-funded international research consortium supporting 15 postdoctoral researchers and has published over 130 scientific articles. His contributions have been recognised through several awards, including the Royal Meteorological Society's Adrian Gill Award. His research brings together climate scientists, meteorologists, policy scholars, epidemiologists and clinical practitioners.

**Shanta research focus**

Protecting and improving the health of communities in England and India from climate-sensitive diseases and environmental hazards. This work combines climate and health data, clinical expertise and policy engagement to develop improved climate–health risk assessments and adaptation strategies that can be evaluated and refined in practice.

## Shanta Fellows in Planetary Health

### Mrinalini Singha
*Climate Interfaces and Immersive Technologies*

Mrinalini Singha is an Assistant Professor of Immersive Arts and Technologies at the University of Bristol's Mumbai Enterprise Campus, where she leads the Runtime Publics Lab. Her research examines computational systems and their relationships with physical, ecological and cultural environments.

**Shanta research focus**

Investigating how climate models and environmental observations can be translated into interactive, immersive and sensory experiences through climate interfaces and metabolic media. Building on previous research at MIT in speculative climate futures and climate justice, her work explores immersive technologies, citizen sensing and participatory design to understand experiences of extreme weather and environmental change in Mumbai and Bristol.

### Dr Tanzim Shaikh
*Statistical Modelling of Climate Risk*

Dr Tanzim Shaikh is an Assistant Professor in Data Science and Statistics at the University of Bristol's Mumbai Enterprise Campus. She holds a PhD in Statistics and specialises in statistical modelling, optimisation and decision-making under uncertainty. Her published research focuses on mathematical models for inventory and supply-chain decisions under uncertain conditions, and she is now extending this expertise to climate-risk assessment and adaptation.

**Shanta research focus**

Investigating uncertainty in extreme rainfall projections for Mumbai and its implications for prioritising climate adaptation. Her research combines statistical methods with climate and spatial datasets to examine how uncertainty can be represented and incorporated into decisions on urban climate resilience. The fellowship also supports collaboration between Bristol and Mumbai, including opportunities for student engagement in applied planetary health research.

### Dr Josephine Walker
*Infectious Disease Modelling and Health Economics*

Dr Josephine Walker is a Senior Lecturer in Health Economic Modelling with over 15 years of experience studying infectious disease transmission, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. Originally trained in ecology and evolutionary biology, her research uses mathematical modelling and interdisciplinary approaches to investigate infectious disease dynamics and evaluate the impact and cost-effectiveness of public health interventions.

Her work has addressed access to diagnosis and treatment, environmental drivers of infectious disease transmission, and the integration of participatory research into modelling frameworks.

**Shanta research focus**

Understanding relationships between environmental conditions, infectious disease risks and public health decision-making, with a particular emphasis on how modelling can inform effective and equitable health interventions.

### Dr Ruby Lieber
*Climate Variability and Health Impact Attribution*

Dr Ruby Lieber is a Senior Research Associate in Climate and Health at the University of Bristol, specialising in tropical climate variability, extreme weather and climate attribution. Her research investigates the contribution of anthropogenic climate change to observed health impacts, particularly heat-related mortality.

**Shanta research focus**

Examining the relative influences of anthropogenic climate change and natural climate variability, including the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO), on extreme heat and associated health outcomes. Her research aims to improve understanding of the climatic drivers of health risks and their implications for assessing future vulnerability and adaptation.
